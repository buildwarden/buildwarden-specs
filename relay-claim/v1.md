# relay-claim, version 1

How a claimant claims a relay: the tunnel, the claim and its reply.

| | |
|---|---|
| Seam | 1, the claim |
| Requirement identifiers | `CLAIM-<n>` |
| Roles | relay, claimant |
| Concept | [The claim protocol](https://buildwarden.github.io/buildwarden/concepts/architecture.html#claim-protocol-relaycfg) in the BuildWarden book |

The key words "MUST", "MUST NOT", "REQUIRED", "SHALL", "SHALL NOT", "SHOULD",
"SHOULD NOT", "RECOMMENDED", "NOT RECOMMENDED", "MAY" and "OPTIONAL" in this
document are to be interpreted as described in BCP 14 (RFC 2119, RFC 8174)
when, and only when, they appear in all capitals. A requirement is a
paragraph or list item that starts with its identifier in bold.

## Scope and roles

A relay starts unconfigured, the same for every build, and waits to be
claimed. The claim gives it everything that belongs to one build: where its
collector is and how to authenticate to it, what to capture, which upstream
roots and resolvers to use, the build's environment record and the segment
it serves the build on. Its reply gives the claimant what the build needs
to trust the relay and to verify its ledger. A relay is claimed once.

Two roles take part:

- The *relay* serves the claim endpoint and answers one claim.
- The *claimant* opens the connections, serves the collector protocol on the
  tunnel when it selects the tunnel, and sends the claim. The claimant is a
  role, defined by what it does; this document names no component for it.
  In the reference implementation it is `driver.Session`, which each driver
  and `cmd/collector` start.

Outside this document:

- Who starts a relay, and how its claim token reaches it before the claim
  (its boot channel), beyond what the Authorization section requires.
- What the relay and the collector exchange on the tunnel, or at a
  `collector_url`: that is the collector protocol, written as the
  specifications `collector-outputs` and `collector-inputs`. This document
  says only how the claim selects where the collector is.
- What the relay serves the build environment on the build link (its
  address lease, DNS, the reserved hosts and the proxy): that is
  `relay-guest-api`. This document says what the claim tells the relay about
  the link and what each mode does with it at the claim.
- Who starts the build environment, and when. The build environment may be
  started as soon as the claim's reply has arrived, by whoever starts it;
  this document gives that start to no role. `relay-guest-api`, section
  Ordering, says what the build environment waits for before its first
  request.

The ledger the relay writes, and the environment record that is its first
entry, are specified in buildwarden/buildwarden under
[`specs/build-ledger/v1.md`](https://github.com/buildwarden/buildwarden/tree/main/specs/build-ledger/v1.md).

## Transport

**CLAIM-1.** The relay MUST serve the claim endpoint as HTTP/1.1 over a byte
stream that the claimant opens. The relay MUST NOT open a connection to the
claimant for any exchange of this document: the tunnel and the claim travel
on connections the claimant opened.

The one binding of that byte stream today is TCP:

**CLAIM-2.** Over TCP, the claim endpoint is a TCP address the claimant can
reach. The relay MUST accept connections on it from the time it is started
until it is claimed or spent. The port is chosen by whoever starts the relay;
the Go relay's default, its `-claim-addr`, is port 8301.

**CLAIM-3.** The claimant MUST retry a connection that fails before the relay
answers (refused, reset, or closed with no HTTP reply), until a limit of its
own, since a relay may still be starting when the claimant first connects.
The reference claimant retries every 250 ms within 90 s for the tunnel and
the claim together; those numbers are its own, not this document's.

The Go relay's claim endpoint is only ever exposed to the host that starts
it: a forwarded or published host-only port, or a host-only switch. In the
reference implementation the build environment is started only once the
claim's reply has arrived, by which time the endpoint has stopped accepting
connections (CLAIM-47).

## Authorization

The relay may be started with a *claim token*, a secret fixed when the relay
is created, which stops another local process from claiming a waiting relay
first.

**CLAIM-4.** Both endpoints, `POST /v1/tunnel` and `POST /v1/claim`, take the
method `POST` only. The relay MUST answer a request with any other method
with `405 Method Not Allowed` and the body `POST only`, and MUST decide this
before it looks at the token.

**CLAIM-5.** A relay started with a claim token MUST require that token in
the request's `Authorization` header on both endpoints, and MUST answer a
request that does not carry it with `401 Unauthorized` and the body
`unauthorized`.

**CLAIM-6.** The relay MUST compare the token it is given with its own in
time that does not depend on where they differ.

**CLAIM-7.** A `401` consumes nothing: after it, the relay MUST still accept
an authorized tunnel and an authorized claim as if the refused request had
not been made.

**CLAIM-8.** A claimant that has a claim token MUST send it on both requests
as `Authorization: Bearer <token>`, the scheme spelled exactly `Bearer`. A
relay MUST accept the token in that form, and need accept the scheme in no
other spelling: the Go relay answers `bearer <token>` with `401`. (The Go
relay also accepts other forms: Known divergences, D8.)

**CLAIM-9.** A relay started without a claim token MUST accept a request on
either endpoint whatever its `Authorization` header holds, or without one.

**CLAIM-10.** The claim token is a value the relay has before it is claimed.
It MUST reach the relay by the means that start it (its boot channel; for
the Go relay, the environment variable `WARDEN_CLAIM_TOKEN`), and it is never
a field of the claim.

## Messages and fields

### The tunnel, `POST /v1/tunnel`

The tunnel carries the collector protocol on a connection the claimant
opened, so that a relay that never dials its host can still reach the
collector.

**CLAIM-11.** A claimant that opens the tunnel MUST send `POST /v1/tunnel`
with the headers `Connection: Upgrade`, `Upgrade: warden-tunnel` and
`Content-Length: 0`, and no body.

**CLAIM-12.** To an authorized tunnel request that carries those headers,
when the relay has accepted no tunnel before and no claim is being
processed or has succeeded, the relay MUST answer `101 Switching Protocols` with the headers
`Connection: Upgrade` and `Upgrade: warden-tunnel`. (The Go relay does not
check the request's upgrade headers: Known divergences, D7.)

**CLAIM-13.** From the end of the `101` reply on, the connection MUST carry
HTTP/2 with prior knowledge and no TLS, with the roles of the connection
reversed: the relay is the HTTP/2 client, which sends the connection
preface, and the claimant is the HTTP/2 server. When the claim selects the
tunnel (CLAIM-22), the relay MUST send every request of the collector
protocol (`collector-outputs` and `collector-inputs`) on it.

The Go relay's requests on the tunnel name the authority `collector.tunnel`,
the host of the value that selects it.

**CLAIM-14.** A claimant that opens the tunnel MUST serve the collector
protocol on it from the `101` on, while its claim is outstanding: the relay
sends collector requests on the tunnel before it answers the claim
(CLAIM-44).

**CLAIM-15.** A relay accepts at most one tunnel for its life. The relay
MUST answer a tunnel request made after it has accepted a tunnel, whether
or not that tunnel is still open, while a claim is being processed, or
after a claim has succeeded, with `409 Conflict` and the body
`tunnel already open`. A relay need not notice that its tunnel has closed
(CLAIM-22).

**CLAIM-16.** The tunnel MUST outlive a refused claim: after a `400` to a
claim, the tunnel stays open and a later claim may select it. It also
outlives the claim endpoint: after the claim's `200` it stays open for the
build.

### The claim, `POST /v1/claim`

**CLAIM-17.** A claimant MUST send `POST /v1/claim` with the header
`Content-Type: application/json` and a body that is one JSON object which
validates against [`v1.config.schema.json`](v1.config.schema.json), and
nothing after it. (The reference claimant sends the claim through the
HTTP proxy its environment names, when the relay's address is not a
loopback one: Known divergences, N31.)

**CLAIM-18.** The relay MUST refuse a body it cannot decode as a claim with
`400 Bad Request` and a body that starts `invalid claim: `. That includes,
at the top level and inside `environment` or `build_link`: a body that is
not JSON; a JSON value that is not an object; a member present with a
value other than `null` that has the wrong type; a byte string (CLAIM-28)
that is not base64; and a member whose name is not a field of this
version, compared without regard to case.

A relay MAY be more lenient than a claimant is required to be (CLAIM-17),
and the Go relay is in these ways: it takes a member whose value is
`null` as absent; it matches a member's name to a field without regard to
case (`COLLECTOR_URL` is `collector_url`); of members with the same name it
takes the last; it ignores whatever follows the object in the body; and it
ignores line breaks inside a base64 value. A claimant does none of these.

**CLAIM-19.** When the relay refuses a member that is not a field of the
version it implements, the reason MUST contain `unknown field "<name>"`,
with the member's name. A claimant MAY read it to recognise a relay that
predates a field it sent.

**CLAIM-20.** A claimant SHOULD leave out an optional field it has no value
for, so that a relay that predates the field accepts the claim.

An example claim that selects the tunnel:

```json v1.config.schema.json
{
  "collector_url": "http://collector.tunnel",
  "collector_token": "5f0c6a1e9b2d4c7f8e3a1b6d9c2e5f80",
  "capture_mode": "headers",
  "build_link": {"prefix": "192.0.2.0/30", "relay": "192.0.2.1", "guest": "192.0.2.2"},
  "upstream_dns": ["198.51.100.53", "203.0.113.53"]
}
```

The fields:

| Field | Type | Required |
|---|---|---|
| `collector_url` | string | Yes |
| `collector_token` | string | No |
| `capture_mode` | string | No |
| `upstream_ca_certs` | array of strings | No |
| `upstream_system_ca` | boolean | No |
| `environment` | object | No |
| `build_link` | object | No |
| `upstream_dns` | array of strings | No |

**CLAIM-21.** `collector_url` is where the relay's collector is. The relay
MUST refuse a claim whose `collector_url` is absent or empty with `400` and
the body `invalid claim: collector_url is required`.

**CLAIM-22.** The value `http://collector.tunnel` selects the tunnel the
claimant opened; the relay compares `collector_url` with it exactly. The
relay MUST refuse a claim that selects the tunnel when it has accepted no
tunnel (CLAIM-15) with `400` and the body
`invalid claim: collector_url selects the tunnel, but none is open`. A
claim that selects a tunnel the relay accepted and that has since closed
MAY be accepted; the relay's start then fails (CLAIM-50).

**CLAIM-23.** Any other value of `collector_url`, `http://collector.tunnel/`
among them, is the collector's base URL, which the relay MUST request
itself, resolving its host through the relay's upstream resolvers
(CLAIM-31, CLAIM-32). The relay removes any trailing `/` from the base URL
and appends the collector protocol's paths, each of which starts `/v1/`.
A value the relay cannot request, and a collector it cannot reach, fail
the relay's start (CLAIM-50; Known divergences, D6). A refusal of the
ledger stream, `PUT /v1/ledger` (`collector-outputs`), need not be seen
before the relay answers the claim: the Go relay sends that request and
goes on without waiting for its reply, so a relay whose collector refuses
the stream MAY answer the claim `200` (Known divergences, D6).

**CLAIM-24.** `collector_token` is the bearer token of the build's session at
the collector. When it is not empty the relay MUST send it on every
collector request as `Authorization: Bearer <token>`; when it is empty or
absent the relay MUST send no `Authorization` header to the collector.

**CLAIM-25.** `capture_mode` chooses what the relay captures of the build's
proxied exchanges: `headers`, `bodies` or `all` (both). The empty string,
`none` and an absent field MUST capture nothing. What a capture is, and how
it reaches the collector, is `collector-outputs`'. (The Go relay accepts any
other value and captures nothing: Known divergences, D5.)

**CLAIM-26.** `upstream_ca_certs` are extra roots for the relay's upstream
TLS, the connections it makes on the build's behalf. Each entry is a PEM
bundle of one or more certificates. When the field holds at least one
entry, the relay MUST trust the certificates in its entries as roots for
those connections. (The Go relay ignores an entry that holds no PEM
certificate: Known divergences, D5.)

**CLAIM-27.** `upstream_system_ca` set to `false` together with a non-empty
`upstream_ca_certs` MUST leave the relay's system roots out of its upstream
TLS, so that only the claim's roots are trusted. `true`, an absent field,
and any value when `upstream_ca_certs` is absent or empty MUST leave the
system roots in.

**CLAIM-28.** `environment` is the build's base environment, which the
relay records as its ledger's environment record and lands with the
collector (`PUT /v1/environment`, `collector-outputs`). It has three
members: `type` (a string, optional), `payload` and `metadata` (each a byte
string in standard base64 with padding, both required). A claimant MUST
send one for which all of these hold, and the relay MUST fail its start
when one of them does not (CLAIM-50; Known divergences, D6):

- `payload` is not empty and decodes to at most 1 MiB (CLAIM-53);
- `payload` is a JSON object that names its own type. When it has a member
  `type`, that member's value is a non-empty string, which is the type.
  When it has none, it has a member `schemaVersion`, and also a member
  `layers`, or a member `manifests`, or a member `mediaType` whose value
  is a string that contains `manifest` or `image.index`; its type is then
  `oci-image/v1`;
- `type`, when it is given and not empty, equals the payload's type;
- `metadata` decodes as one CBOR data item, a map whose keys are text
  strings, with nothing after it.

A relay MAY take a `metadata` that is the CBOR `null` or `undefined` as an
empty map, MAY accept a tag on the map, and MAY take the last of entries
with the same key; the Go relay does all three. A claimant sends a map
with no tag and no repeated key.

The relay MUST also fail its start when the collector answers
`PUT /v1/environment` with any status but `200`, or that request fails
(CLAIM-50).

The types and their payloads are those of the ledger's environment record
(`specs/build-ledger/v1.md` in buildwarden/buildwarden).

**CLAIM-29.** `build_link` is the private segment between the relay and the
build environment, with three members, each a string: `prefix`, the
segment's network in CIDR form; `relay`, the relay's address on it; and
`guest`, the build environment's. The relay MUST refuse a claim whose
`build_link` breaks one of these rules with `400` and a body that starts
`invalid claim: build_link: `, whatever its mode:

- every address is IPv4;
- `prefix` is its own network address, with a length from 8 to 30;
- `relay` and `guest` are inside `prefix`, are neither its network nor its
  broadcast address, and differ;
- no address is in 0.0.0.0/8, loopback, link-local, multicast or class E
  (240.0.0.0/4).

**CLAIM-30.** `upstream_dns`, when present, is the list of resolvers the
relay uses. The relay MUST refuse a claim whose `upstream_dns` breaks one
of these rules with `400` and a body that starts
`invalid claim: upstream_dns: `, whatever its mode: the list is not empty;
each entry is an IPv4 address, written without a port (port 53 is
implied); no entry is the unspecified address, a multicast address or the
broadcast address 255.255.255.255; and, when the claim has a `build_link`,
no entry is inside its `prefix`.

**CLAIM-31.** When `upstream_dns` is present, the relay MUST resolve every
name it resolves through exactly the listed servers and no other, asking
them in list order: the build's DNS queries it forwards (`relay-guest-api`,
section Messages and fields), the hosts of its upstream connections, and
the host of a `collector_url` that is not the tunnel.

**CLAIM-32.** When `upstream_dns` is absent, the relay's resolvers MUST be
the nameservers of its own platform resolver configuration (for the Go
relay, `/etc/resolv.conf`), leaving out the relay's own address,
127.0.0.1, and any address inside its build link. That list may hold
addresses the claim's own field may not (CLAIM-30): the Go relay keeps an
IPv6 nameserver, and a loopback address other than 127.0.0.1, such as
127.0.0.53 or Docker's 127.0.0.11. When none is left, or the configuration
cannot be read, the relay MUST refuse the claim with `400` and a body that
starts `claim refused: ` and names `upstream_dns`.

### The reply

**CLAIM-33.** To a claim it accepts, the relay MUST answer `200 OK` with
`Content-Type: application/json` and a body that validates against
[`v1.identity.schema.json`](v1.identity.schema.json), with these members:

- `ca_cert`: the PEM encoding of the X.509 certificate of the certificate
  authority this relay created for the build, which signs every
  certificate the relay presents to the build environment
  (`relay-guest-api`);
- `ledger_public_key`: the Ed25519 public key that signs this build's
  ledger, as 64 lower-case hexadecimal digits.

{

**CLAIM-34.** A claimant MUST treat a `200` whose `ca_cert` is absent or
empty as a failed claim. It MUST ignore members of the reply it does not
know.

**CLAIM-35.** A claimant MUST treat any reply to the tunnel request other
than `101`, and any reply to the claim other than `200`, as final for that
request: it does not retry it.

## Modes

How the build's traffic reaches a relay differs between the reference
implementation's three modes (RELAY-04 in buildwarden/buildwarden), and so
does what the relay does with the claim's `build_link`:

- *vm*: the relay is the init of its own virtual machine and serves the
  build on a kernel interface, its *build interface*;
- *host*: the relay runs on the host and serves the build on a frame link,
  Ethernet frames on a socket it inherits, through a network stack of its
  own;
- *container*: the relay runs in a container whose address the container
  runtime assigns.

**CLAIM-36.** A vm-mode relay MUST refuse a claim whose `build_link` another
of its interfaces already uses, with `400` and a body that starts
`claim refused: `: another interface holds an address inside `prefix`, or
has a route whose destination lies inside `prefix` or equals it. A route
that strictly contains `prefix`, such as the default route, is not a use.

**CLAIM-37.** A vm-mode relay that accepts a claim with a `build_link` MUST
replace the IPv4 addresses of its build interface with `relay` at the
length of `prefix`, and serve the build environment's address lease for
`guest` on that interface (`relay-guest-api`, section Messages and
fields), before it answers the claim.

**CLAIM-38.** A vm-mode relay claimed without a `build_link` MUST keep the
address already on its build interface as its own address, with that
address's network as its build link. When the interface has no IPv4
address, it MUST refuse the claim with `400` and a body that starts
`claim refused: `.

The address already on the build interface may be one an earlier claim
set. The Go relay addresses the interface (CLAIM-37) before it looks for
its resolvers (CLAIM-32), and a refusal at that later step stops the
address lease but leaves the address: a later claim without a
`build_link` keeps the refused claim's `relay` address and its network.
CLAIM-48 allows this; the relay is still claimable.

**CLAIM-39.** A host-mode relay MUST build its link (its network stack, its
address and the build environment's lease) on the claim's `build_link`,
and, without one, on a segment fixed when it was started (for the Go relay,
`-subnet`, by default 10.100.0.0/30 with the relay at .1 and the build
environment at .2).

**CLAIM-40.** A container-mode relay MUST refuse a claim that carries a
`build_link` with `400` and a body that starts `claim refused: `.

**CLAIM-41.** A relay that has a build link, a vm-mode or host-mode relay
with or without the field, MUST NOT open an upstream connection on the
build's behalf to an address inside that link. What the build environment
sees when it asks for one is `relay-guest-api`'s.

## Ordering

**CLAIM-42.** The relay MUST look at a request in this order: its method
(CLAIM-4), its token (CLAIM-5), whether a claim is being processed or has
succeeded (CLAIM-15, CLAIM-46), and only then its body.

**CLAIM-43.** A claimant that selects the tunnel MUST have opened it, and
read its `101`, before it sends the claim.

**CLAIM-44.** The relay MUST answer a claim with `200` only after all of
these:

- its build segment is configured: its address, and the address lease
  where it serves one (CLAIM-37, CLAIM-39);
- it has sent `PUT /v1/ledger`, the ledger stream, to the collector
  (`collector-outputs`), which it need not have had a reply to (CLAIM-23);
- when the claim has an `environment`, its payload has been landed with the
  collector, `PUT /v1/environment`, and the collector has answered it
  `200` (CLAIM-28);
- it has created the certificate authority whose certificate is the reply's
  `ca_cert`;
- it has begun starting its build-facing listeners (CLAIM-45);
- it has sent `POST /v1/ready` to the collector (`collector-inputs`) and has
  its reply or its failure. A failed `ready` MUST NOT fail the claim.

**CLAIM-45.** A host-mode relay MUST have its build-facing listeners serving
before it answers the claim. A vm-mode or container-mode relay MAY still be
binding its listeners' ports when it answers; a port it then fails to bind
ends the relay after the `200` (Known divergences, D19).

**CLAIM-46.** A relay accepts one claim. It MUST answer a claim made while
another is being processed with `409 Conflict` and the body
`already claimed`. A claim is being processed from the time the relay has
read its headers until it has answered it, its body's reading included
(Limits). It MUST NOT accept a claim made after one has succeeded: it
either answers it `409` with the same body or closes the connection
without a reply, and it accepts no new connection (CLAIM-47). The Go
relay closes the connections it was serving once it has sent the `200`,
so a claim sent after it on one of them, kept alive or idle, is closed
with no reply.

**CLAIM-47.** After it has answered a claim with `200`, the relay MUST stop
accepting connections on the claim endpoint. The tunnel, if one is open,
stays open (CLAIM-16).

## Errors

The relay's answers to a request it does not accept, in the order it looks
for them (CLAIM-42):

| Status | Body | When | The relay after it |
|---|---|---|---|
| `405` | `POST only` | Any method but `POST` (CLAIM-4) | Unchanged |
| `401` | `unauthorized` | No valid token (CLAIM-5) | Unchanged (CLAIM-7) |
| `409` | `tunnel already open` | A second tunnel, open or not the first, or one during or after a claim (CLAIM-15) | Unchanged |
| `409` | `already claimed` | A claim during or after another (CLAIM-46); after a `200` the connection may instead be closed with no reply | Unchanged |
| `400` | `invalid claim: ...` | A claim that does not decode, or whose fields break a rule of this document (CLAIM-18, CLAIM-21, CLAIM-22, CLAIM-29, CLAIM-30) | Claimable (CLAIM-48) |
| `400` | `claim refused: ...` | A claim the relay's mode cannot apply (CLAIM-32, CLAIM-36, CLAIM-38, CLAIM-40) | Claimable (CLAIM-48), but see D17 |
| `500` | `relay start failed: ...` | An accepted claim the relay could not start with (CLAIM-50) | Spent |

**CLAIM-48.** A claim answered `400` MUST leave the relay claimable: the same
relay MUST accept a later valid claim, with the tunnel that was open still
open. The one exception is a host-mode relay that refused a claim for want
of a usable resolver (CLAIM-32): what it does with any later claim is not
specified, and it MAY refuse it, fail it, or close the connection with no
reply (Known divergences, D17).

**CLAIM-49.** The body of a `400` for a claim the relay's mode cannot apply
MUST start `claim refused: ` and name what the relay could not apply.

**CLAIM-50.** A relay that accepted a claim and could not start with it MUST
answer `500 Internal Server Error` with a body that starts
`relay start failed: `, and is then spent: it MUST accept no further claim,
and it ends. An unusable `environment` or `collector_url` is such a failure
(CLAIM-22, CLAIM-23, CLAIM-28; Known divergences, D6), as is a failure of
the relay itself.

**CLAIM-51.** The relay MUST send every refusal of this document with a
`Content-Type` whose media type is `text/plain`, parameters such as
`charset` allowed, and its reason on one line. (The Go relay sends
`text/plain; charset=utf-8` and adds `X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff`.)

What is not part of the protocol. A claimant sends nothing but the two
requests above, and a relay MAY answer anything else as it will. The Go
relay's HTTP server answers a request for any other path `404` with the
body `404 page not found`, `/v1/claim/` and `/v1/tunnel/` among them;
redirects a path that is not clean, such as `/v1//claim` or
`/v1/x/../claim`, with `307` to its clean form; answers `OPTIONS *` with
`200`; and answers `HEAD` with `405` and no body. It ignores a request's
query, its `Content-Type` and its `Host` header, and of two
`Authorization` headers it reads the first.

## Limits

**CLAIM-52.** The relay MUST accept a claim body of up to 16 MiB. It MUST
refuse, as a body it cannot decode (CLAIM-18), one whose JSON object does
not end within its first 16 MiB. A claimant MUST NOT send a claim body
larger than 16 MiB.

**CLAIM-53.** The environment's payload is at most 1 MiB once decoded. A
larger one is unusable (CLAIM-28).

Other numbers in this protocol are the implementations' own and not this
document's: how long a claimant tries to reach a relay (CLAIM-3), and how
long the Go relay waits for a request's headers (10 s) and for its reply to
a claim to be sent before it closes the endpoint (5 s).

The Go relay sets no limit on how long it reads a claim's body. A claim
whose body stalls is being processed (CLAIM-46) until its connection
closes or its body ends: meanwhile every other claim is answered `409`
`already claimed` and every tunnel request `409` `tunnel already open`.

## Versioning

The paths name the protocol's major version, `/v1/`, which is this
document's: `v1.md` is version 1. While this specification's releases are
`v0.x.y`, the document is edited in place and may still change
incompatibly; from `relay-claim/v1.0.0` on, version 1 changes only
compatibly, and an incompatible change is a new document `v2.md` with
paths of its own.

Within version 1, a new field is optional. A relay that predates it
refuses a claim that carries it (CLAIM-18, CLAIM-19), which a claimant can
tell from the reason; a claimant that has no value for it leaves it out
(CLAIM-20), and so still claims a relay that predates it. The reply may
gain members, which a claimant ignores (CLAIM-34).

## Conformance

A relay conforms to this version when it meets every requirement of the
relay: CLAIM-1, CLAIM-2, CLAIM-4 to CLAIM-7, CLAIM-8 (the relay's part),
CLAIM-9, CLAIM-10, CLAIM-12, CLAIM-13, CLAIM-15, CLAIM-16, CLAIM-18,
CLAIM-19 (the relay's part), CLAIM-21 to CLAIM-33, the requirements of
its mode among CLAIM-36 to CLAIM-41, CLAIM-42, CLAIM-44 to CLAIM-53 (the
relay's part of CLAIM-52). A relay that is none of the three modes states
which mode's requirements it meets.

A claimant conforms to this version when it meets every requirement of the
claimant: CLAIM-3, CLAIM-8 (the claimant's part), CLAIM-11, CLAIM-14,
CLAIM-17, CLAIM-20, CLAIM-34, CLAIM-35, CLAIM-43 and the claimant's part of
CLAIM-52. A claimant that never selects the tunnel has no tunnel to open,
and CLAIM-11, CLAIM-14 and CLAIM-43 do not apply to it.

## Known divergences

Places where the reference implementation differs from a rule or a comment
of its own repository, or from the rest of this protocol. Each is what the
code does, and the requirements above allow it where they name it. The
numbers are those of the design that wrote this document,
`docs/design/seam-specs.md` in buildwarden/buildwarden, under "Divergences
found". N5 to N11, N26 and N31 are not yet in that table; the N numbers are
shared by the six documents, and the others are those of
`bootstrap-config`, `executor-initialize`, the collector documents and
`relay-guest-api`. The line numbers are those of
buildwarden/buildwarden at `623131a`, where the files they name are as they
were at `3cb0b2b`.

### D5: values that cannot be used are accepted

The Go relay accepts a `capture_mode` that is none of the values of
CLAIM-25, and captures nothing. It ignores an entry of `upstream_ca_certs`
that holds no PEM certificate; with `upstream_system_ca` `false`, a claim
whose entries hold none leaves the relay trusting no upstream at all. Both
differ from GLOBAL-10 in buildwarden/buildwarden, under which a provided
value that cannot be used is an error.

### D6: some invalid fields spend the relay

An unusable `environment` (an empty payload, one over 1 MiB, a payload that
names no type, a `type` that differs from the payload's, metadata that is
not a CBOR map) and an unusable `collector_url` (one the relay cannot
request, a collector it cannot reach, a tunnel that has closed) are found
only while the relay starts, after it has accepted the claim. The Go relay
answers them `500 relay start failed: ...` and is spent (CLAIM-50), where
every other invalid field is a `400` that leaves the relay claimable
(CLAIM-48).

A collector that refuses the ledger stream is not among them, though the
design's table names it there. The Go relay starts `PUT /v1/ledger` in the
background and writes the ledger's header into the request body; the
header is taken before a refusal arrives, so the start goes on, `ready` is
sent and the claim is answered `200`. Seen against the Go relay built from
buildwarden/buildwarden at `3cb0b2b`, with a collector that answered
`PUT /v1/ledger` `401` at once and after 200 ms: the start succeeded both
times and the collector then received `POST /v1/ready` (CLAIM-23).

### D7: the tunnel's upgrade headers are not checked

The Go relay answers `101` to any authorized `POST /v1/tunnel`, whether or
not it carries `Connection: Upgrade` and `Upgrade: warden-tunnel`, and then
speaks HTTP/2 on the connection. The reference claimant always sends them
(CLAIM-11).

### D8: the token without its scheme

Besides `Authorization: Bearer <token>`, the Go relay accepts the token with
no scheme: it removes a leading `Bearer`, if there is one, and any white
space around what is left, and compares the rest, so `Bearertok` and
`Bearer   tok  ` are accepted for the token `tok`. The prefix it removes is
case-sensitive: `bearer tok` is refused `401`. Every claimant sends the
`Bearer` form (CLAIM-8). The collector's side of the same token handling,
which differs from the relay's, is in `collector-outputs` and
`collector-inputs`.

### D17: a host-mode refusal for want of a resolver leaves the relay unusable

A host-mode relay builds its link, its network stack on the inherited
frame socket, before it looks for its resolvers. When a claim has no
`upstream_dns` and the relay's `/etc/resolv.conf` names no usable
nameserver, the Go relay answers `400 claim refused: upstream_dns is absent
...` and releases the claim (CLAIM-32), but in undoing its link it closes
the frame socket's descriptor. A later claim builds its link on that
descriptor number, which the relay no longer holds and may have reused.
Seen against the Go relay built from buildwarden/buildwarden at
`3cb0b2b`, with `-mode host` and a `/etc/resolv.conf` naming only
127.0.0.1: the first claim was answered `400` as above; the second, with
`upstream_dns`, was accepted on a connection that had been given the same
descriptor number, so the relay built its link on the claimant's own
connection, wrote an Ethernet frame (a gratuitous ARP) to the claimant on
it, failed its start, closed that connection with no HTTP reply and exited.
This differs from "a refused claim leaves the relay claimable", which the
vm and container modes keep. CLAIM-48 leaves the relay's answer to any
later claim unspecified, so a suite asserts only the first `400`.

### D19: the reply before the ports are bound

In vm and container mode, the Go relay answers the claim, and sends
`POST /v1/ready`, once its listeners' goroutines have started, not once
their ports are bound. A port that then fails to bind ends the relay after
the `200`, and a failure to bind UDP port 53 is not reported at all. A
host-mode relay has its listeners before it answers (CLAIM-45). `ready`
means "the relay is up" (`collector-inputs`).

### N5: a refused ledger stream does not fail the start

Not yet in the design's table (found 3a).

The design's row for D6 has a collector that refuses the ledger stream
among the `collector_url`s that fail the start. The Go relay does not see
that refusal before it answers: `Start` opens `PUT /v1/ledger` and writes
the ledger's header into the request's body (`relay/relay.go:211-220`,
`relay/collector_client.go:154`), and the header is taken before the
collector's reply arrives, so the claim is answered `200` and `ready` is
sent to a collector that refused the ledger. CLAIM-23 and CLAIM-50 allow
it, and D6 above says what was seen. Seen against the Go relay at
`3cb0b2b`, with a collector that answered `PUT /v1/ledger` `401` at once
and after 200 ms: `Start` returned no error both times and the collector
then received `POST /v1/ready`. A very fast refusal may still fail the
start; the race was not narrowed further.

### N6: the tunnel is never released

Not yet in the design's table (found 3a).

The Go relay keeps the first tunnel it accepts for its life
(`relaycfg/relaycfg.go:238-251`: `s.tunnel` is set at `:249` and never
cleared) and does not notice that it has closed. A second `POST /v1/tunnel`
is therefore `409` whether or not the first is still open (CLAIM-15), and
a claim that selects a tunnel that has closed is accepted and then fails
the start with `500`, spending the relay (CLAIM-22, CLAIM-50), where a
claim that selects no tunnel at all is a `400` that leaves it claimable.
The design's D6 row names neither. Seen with a stand-in tunnel transport
that fails every request: the start failed with `initializing ledger:
write header: ... tunnel closed`; a real HTTP/2 tunnel closed by its
claimant was not tried.

### N7: the claim's body is decoded leniently

Not yet in the design's table (found 3a).

`handleClaim` decodes the body with `encoding/json` and
`DisallowUnknownFields` (`relaycfg/relaycfg.go:268-270`), which takes a
member whose value is `null` as absent, matches names without regard to
case, takes the last of two members with the same name, stops at the end
of the first value so that what follows is ignored, and decodes base64
with line breaks in it (CLAIM-18). A claimant may do none of these
(CLAIM-17). This differs from GLOBAL-10 in buildwarden/buildwarden as the
D5 row reads it: a value that cannot be used as given is accepted. Seen
against the Go relay at `3cb0b2b`: case-variant names, `null` members, a
duplicate `collector_url` (the last, `http://d`, was used), trailing
garbage and a second object after the first, and `"e3\n0="` as base64 were
all accepted; `e30` and `e30-` were refused.

### N8: the environment's metadata is decoded leniently

Not yet in the design's table (found 3a).

`recordEnvironment` decodes `metadata` with `cbor.Unmarshal` into a map
(`relay/relay.go:476-478`), which takes the CBOR `null` and `undefined` as
an empty map, accepts a tag on the map, and keeps the last of repeated keys
(CLAIM-28). The design's D6 row has "metadata that is not a CBOR map" fail
the start; these are not maps and do not. Seen against the Go relay at
`3cb0b2b`: `null`, `undefined`, a tagged map and a map with a repeated key
were accepted; an empty item, trailing bytes, an array, and integer and
byte-string keys were refused.

### N9: a stalled claim body holds the relay

Not yet in the design's table (found 3a).

The claim endpoint's server sets `ReadHeaderTimeout` only
(`relaycfg/relaycfg.go:353`), and `handleClaim` marks the relay claimed
before it reads the body (`relaycfg/relaycfg.go:263-268`). A claimant whose
body stalls keeps the claim "being processed" until its connection closes:
every other claim is `409 already claimed` and every tunnel request `409
tunnel already open` meanwhile (Limits, CLAIM-46). Nothing in the design
records a limit on this or its absence. Seen against the Go relay at
`3cb0b2b`: a second claim was still answered `409` 11 s into a stalled
body, and the claim was released once that connection closed.

### N10: the resolver default keeps addresses the claim may not name

Not yet in the design's table (found 3a).

With no `upstream_dns`, `ResolvConfServers` (`relay/relay.go:407-428`)
keeps every nameserver of `/etc/resolv.conf` but 127.0.0.1, the relay's own
address and addresses inside the build link: an IPv6 nameserver, 127.0.0.53
and Docker's 127.0.0.11 are kept (CLAIM-32), though `upstream_dns` refuses
each of them in a claim (CLAIM-30). The default and the field therefore
differ in what they let the relay use. Read from the code; not run.

### N11: a refused vm-mode claim leaves its address on the build interface

Not yet in the design's table (found 3a).

In vm mode, a claim with a `build_link` flushes the build interface and
puts the claim's relay address on it (`cmd/relay/vmlink.go:60-66`) before
the relay looks for its resolvers (`cmd/relay/main.go:104-130`). A refusal
at that later step stops the address lease but leaves the address, and a
later claim with no `build_link` adopts it as the relay's own, with its
network as the build link (`cmd/relay/vmlink.go:77-89`; the paragraph after
CLAIM-38). This differs from "a refused claim leaves the relay claimable"
(`build-link-subnet`) in the way D17 does for host mode: the relay is still
claimable, but not as it was. CLAIM-48 allows it. Read from the code; not
run, for want of a vm build interface on the host that reviewed it.

### N26: the scheme `Bearer` is matched in its case

Not yet in the design's table (found 3a).

The claim endpoint removes a leading `Bearer`, exactly so spelt, from the
`Authorization` field (`relaycfg/relaycfg.go:223`), so `bearer <token>` is
refused with `401` (CLAIM-8). This differs from RFC 9110, under which an
authentication scheme is case-insensitive, and goes beyond D8, which
records only that the token is accepted with no scheme. Seen against the
Go relay at `3cb0b2b`: `bearer` got `401`.

### N31: the reference claimant's claim follows the proxy environment

Not yet in the design's table (found 8a).

The reference claimant opens the tunnel on a TCP connection it dials
itself (`relaycfg/tunnel.go:79-81`), but sends the claim with an
`http.Client` on Go's default transport (`relaycfg/relaycfg.go:381`,
`:393`), which takes its proxy from `HTTP_PROXY` and `NO_PROXY` and leaves
loopback addresses unproxied. With `HTTP_PROXY` set and a relay at an
address that is not a loopback one, the claim, its claim token in
`Authorization` and its `collector_token` in the body, goes to that proxy,
and the proxy's answer is taken as the relay's (CLAIM-17, CLAIM-35). The
tunnel and the claim then reach different hosts. This differs from the
rest of this protocol, whose two requests travel on connections the
claimant opened to the relay (CLAIM-1). Seen against `relaycfg.Claim` built
from buildwarden/buildwarden at `623131a`, with `HTTP_PROXY` naming a
listener on 127.0.0.1 and a relay address of 192.0.2.1:8301: the listener
received `POST http://192.0.2.1:8301/v1/claim` with
`Authorization: Bearer tok`, answered `502`, and `Claim` returned
`claim rejected: HTTP 502: proxy`. No suite case sets a proxy for a
claimant under test.
